Data

Bring in your own numbers. Volumes come from SQL Server, the offer file and the MIS workbook are uploaded here. Everything stays on this computer and is never added to GitHub.

  1. Volumes from SQL Server

  2. Offer file

    The monthly offer workbook with list prices, trade margin and offers per customer and SKU. Excel or .xlsb.

  3. MIS workbook

    The P&L MIS with costs by SKU, sales office and customer group. It can be large, so uploading may take a minute.

  4. Build the dataset

    Uses the average of the months you tick as the baseline. Reading the MIS takes a few minutes. The simulator and P&L switch to the new data when it finishes.

What was built

Costs against the MIS

Each attributed cost as a share of net revenue, next to the same share in the MIS. The MIS covers more customers and months than the offer file, so some gap is expected. A large gap on one line shows where to look.

Revenue per pack against the MIS

COGS, S&D and over/under come from the MIS per pack, but revenue per pack comes from billing. If the two differ, those costs look smaller or larger as a share of revenue and the margin moves with them. A gap of +3% means billing sells the same packs for 3% more than the MIS shows.

Billed price against the offer file

For each chain and SKU, the price the offer file implies (MRP less trade margin less offer, ex-GST) next to what billing charged. Net revenue always follows billing, so a gap here does not move margin. It moves money between promo and other deductions. Common causes: an offer that changed during the month, several offer rows for one customer and SKU, or a scheme the offer file does not hold.
